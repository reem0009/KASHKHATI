# KASHKHATI — Full Project & Product Brief for OpenCode

**Purpose of this document:** This is the complete working brief for the KASHKHATI project. It defines the business concept, brand, cultural positioning, product structure, website architecture, validation demo, quiz, personalization logic, digital products, visual system, and the implementation plan for OpenCode.

The central idea has evolved from a generic beauty-guide shop into a **personalized cultural beauty library**.

> **The product is discovering what genuinely suits you through your features, lifestyle, and cultural identity.**

KASHKHATI should not impose one universal Western beauty standard. It should help each woman understand her own features and preferences, then explore beauty and style resources that respect her lifestyle, modesty preferences, and cultural context.

---

# 1. The Business Idea

## 1.1 The Problem

Women and girls often struggle to figure out what beauty and style choices actually work for **them**.

They search through hundreds of videos, Pinterest boards, TikToks, tutorials, and trend cycles. Professional color analysis and styling can be expensive, while hiring a stylist to rebuild a wardrobe is inaccessible to many people.

The problem is not a lack of beauty information.

There is **too much information**, and much of it assumes a narrow set of Western beauty and fashion norms.

This becomes especially apparent for women whose needs are poorly represented by mainstream beauty platforms, including:

- Muslim women who wear hijab or prefer modest fashion
- Women who want traditional or culturally informed styling
- Women from communities whose beauty practices are rarely represented
- Women who want to incorporate heritage into contemporary style
- Women who simply want guidance based on their own features rather than trends

The result is research fatigue, confusion, wasted money, and the feeling that beauty advice was not designed for them.

---

# 2. The KASHKHATI Solution

KASHKHATI is a **personalized beauty and style platform** built around a simple loop:

**Discover → Understand → Explore → Apply**

The user begins with a short profile and personalization quiz.

KASHKHATI uses that information to create a personalized:

# Beauty Blueprint

The Blueprint acts as a roadmap through the larger KASHKHATI library.

Instead of giving everyone the same beauty advice, KASHKHATI helps the user discover:

- colors that complement her
- makeup approaches that suit her features
- hair guidance appropriate to her hair type
- clothing silhouettes that work for her proportions
- fragrance families aligned with her preferences
- modest-fashion options when relevant
- cultural style resources relevant to her selected identity or interests
- routines adapted to her lifestyle and climate

The long-term product is therefore not simply a collection of PDFs.

It is a **personalization engine connected to a curated digital library**.

# 4. Cultural Positioning

## 4.1 The New Direction

KASHKHATI is designed to accommodate women from a broad range of cultural backgrounds, including Muslim women and women from Indigenous and other historically underrepresented communities.

The platform should be **culture-aware rather than culture-assuming**.

A user's culture, heritage, religion, modesty preferences, and relationship with traditional clothing should never be assumed from appearance.

Where relevant, the platform asks the user what she wants.

For example:

- Do you wear hijab?
- Do you regularly wear traditional clothing?
- Would you like your recommendations to incorporate elements of your cultural heritage?
- Do you prefer traditional, contemporary, or fusion styling?
- Are there cultural or religious considerations you want KASHKHATI to respect?

These choices are optional wherever possible.

---

## 4.2 Cultural Representation Principle

KASHKHATI must **never treat cultures as costumes**.

Cultural chapters and recommendations should be presented as:

> **well-researched style resources, not exhaustive representations of entire cultures.**

A chapter about a culture should never imply that one outfit, garment, beauty practice, or aesthetic represents everyone in that culture.

Cultures contain enormous internal diversity based on:

- region
- ethnicity
- religion
- generation
- community
- personal preference
- historical period
- contemporary fashion

Therefore:

- Avoid generic "African style" or "Asian beauty" stereotypes.
- Be specific about regions and communities whenever appropriate.
- Distinguish heritage from contemporary fashion.
- Avoid presenting ceremonial clothing as everyday clothing without context.
- Avoid using sacred or culturally significant items as generic decoration.
- Research cultural terminology carefully.
- Where possible, involve people from the relevant community in content development.
- Give contributors and cultural experts credit.
- Treat cultural content as educational and curated rather than authoritative over an entire population.

KASHKHATI should say:

> "A researched guide to selected styling traditions and contemporary interpretations."

rather than:

> "The complete guide to [culture]."

# 12. Typography

Reference image:
reference_images/kashkhati_palette_typography

## Latin / English

Use:

- **Playfair Display** — headings, major editorial statements, logo treatment
- **Montserrat** — body copy, navigation, forms, buttons, UI labels

Both can be loaded from Google Fonts.

## Arabic

The Arabic version should use fonts that preserve the same editorial, elegant, and premium character while remaining highly readable.

Use:

- **Noto Naskh Arabic** — primary Arabic editorial/headline font; ideal for longer headings, guide titles, quotations, and culturally oriented editorial content
- **Noto Kufi Arabic** — Arabic UI and sans-serif font; use for navigation, buttons, labels, forms, body/UI text, and compact interface elements

### Arabic typography rules

- Use **Noto Naskh Arabic** when the design needs an elegant, literary, or editorial feel.
- Use **Noto Kufi Arabic** for interface elements where clarity and modernity are more important.
- Do not force an Arabic font to imitate the Latin typography exactly; the Arabic type system should feel native while maintaining the same brand personality.
- Support full **RTL (right-to-left)** layout rather than simply aligning English content to the right.
- Mirror directional UI elements where appropriate, including navigation, breadcrumbs, arrows, and multi-step quiz layouts.
- Keep Arabic line-height generous, especially in long-form guides.
- Test Arabic and Latin typography together so mixed-language pages remain visually balanced.
- Avoid overly decorative Arabic display fonts for core content; KASHKHATI should remain refined and readable rather than ornamental.

### Suggested font pairing

**English:**
> Playfair Display + Montserrat

**Arabic:**
> Noto Naskh Arabic + Noto Kufi Arabic

The Arabic and English systems should feel like two expressions of the same KASHKHATI identity rather than separate brands.

# 33. Ethical Cultural Content Standard

Before publishing a cultural resource, ask:

1. Is the community clearly identified?
2. Are we avoiding broad generalizations?
3. Is the information researched?
4. Are the images appropriate?
5. Are sacred/ceremonial elements treated respectfully?
6. Are we distinguishing contemporary fashion from tradition?
7. Have we consulted people from the relevant community where possible?
8. Are we avoiding language that implies cultural authority?
9. Is the resource framed as a starting point rather than a complete representation?

This is essential to the brand.

# 43. Major Product Risks

## Risk 1 — Trying to represent every culture

Do not launch with 50 cultural categories.

Start with a small number of carefully researched resources and expand.

Quality matters more than quantity.

## Risk 2 — Tokenism

Simply placing women from different cultures in the hero does not make the product culturally inclusive.

The actual recommendations and library need to respect those contexts.

## Risk 3 — Stereotyping

Never infer someone's culture from appearance.

Culture should come from user selection.

## Risk 4 — Too many guides

A flat library can become chaotic.

That is why the chapter system and personalized recommendations are important.

## Risk 5 — Beauty claims

Recommendations should be framed as guidance rather than objective truths.

Avoid:

> "This is the correct style for your body."

Prefer:

> "These silhouettes may create the balance you prefer."

## Risk 6 — AI overclaiming

The AI should not pretend to understand someone's culture perfectly.

It should recommend curated resources and clearly communicate limitations.
